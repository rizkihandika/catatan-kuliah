## Slide 3

Expressing Time and Chronological Order: communicate events and actions in relation to time. For example, using the simple past tense (e.g., "She studied") allows us to convey an action that occurred and ended in the past, while the present continuous tense (e.g., "He is playing") indicates an action happening at the present moment.
Conveying Relationships and Conditions: express relationships between different actions or conditions. For example, using the past perfect tense (e.g., "I had studied") indicates that an action was completed before another past action, providing a deeper understanding of the sequence of events. 
Communicating States and Habits: describe ongoing states or habitual actions. By using the present continuous tense (e.g., "She is reading"), we can convey an action in progress at the current moment. Similarly, the present simple tense (e.g., "They play tennis every weekend") allows us to express habits or general truths.
